# Milestone 0 threat model

Status: proposed controls and future verification requirements; no claim that these controls are implemented. See [product specification](product-spec.md) and [acceptance scenarios](acceptance-tests.md).

## Assets and trust boundaries
Protect prospect identity/contact details, private replies, research evidence, drafts, approvals, proposal terms, OAuth/API credentials, suppression records, quota counters and the operator's sending reputation. Authenticated browser input is still untrusted. Boundaries are browser → server/domain services → workspace-scoped Postgres; n8n → authenticated job APIs; provider → webhook ingestion; research sites/AI → validated data; dispatch → irreversible provider handoff.

Attackers include unauthenticated callers, a malicious workspace member, compromised provider/source content and replaying clients. Ordinary retries, crashes, stale jobs and provider outages can cause equivalent harm. V1 has no AI authority to send, change prices, accept contracts, clear stops or grant membership.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Prompt injection in websites, Apollo fields, replies or model output | Treat all external text as data; no instruction/tool authority. Allowlist AI actions and output fields; validate with Zod. Ground factual draft claims in retained sources and require exact human approval. | T08, T09, T31 |
| Fabricated, stale or misleading personalization | Store URL, relevant excerpt, retrieval time and observation-to-claim links; show evidence in review. Unknown facts stay unknown; conflicting or unavailable evidence needs review. | T08, T09 |
| SSRF through research URLs/redirects | Server fetcher allows only approved HTTP(S) destinations/ports; reject local/private/link-local/metadata IPs across IPv4/IPv6, credentials in URLs and unsafe redirects. Revalidate every resolved destination/redirect against DNS rebinding; bound time, bytes and redirect count. | T10 |
| Secrets or private content leaked to browser, logs, model or exports | Server-only secret storage and scoped credentials; encrypted per-connection tokens; redact logs/workflow exports. Minimize AI inputs; never include secrets. Rotate/revoke compromised connections and stop their jobs. | T05, T31 |
| Cross-workspace reads/writes or worker confused deputy | Every business row is workspace-owned; migrations enable RLS on every public table. Check authenticated membership at every API/worker boundary, including joins and object references; privileged workers verify scope independently of RLS. | T03, T04 |
| Membership/role escalation | Clients cannot self-add membership, assign privileged roles or trust a supplied workspace ID. Protect membership policy and administrative writes without recursive-policy escape; server authority must originate from verified identity. | T03, T04 |
| Webhook forgery/replay | Verify authenticity using the chosen provider's documented mechanism and raw body requirements before accepting effects; apply freshness/replay checks where supported. Durable event keys are provider/connection-scoped and transactionally deduplicated. No invented signature/header/schema assumptions. | T11, T12 |
| Duplicate, delayed or out-of-order events | Persist event receipt plus deduplication key; atomically update domain state/outbox. Preserve terminal stop latches; reconcile current booking/provider state rather than blindly applying arrival order. Unknown identities enter quarantine/review. | T12, T13, T23 |
| Reply/unsubscribe/booking races with queued send | Stop ingestion and dispatch eligibility use the same per-contact serialization; final checks immediately precede provider handoff. Stop wins before handoff, cancels remaining jobs and invalidates eligibility regardless of approval or intent. Audit residual after-handoff race. | T17, T18, T22 |
| Provider accepts send but response is lost | Persist dispatch identity and quota reservation before handoff; use documented idempotency if available. Mark outcome uncertain; reconcile authoritative history/status. No blind retry or follow-up; manual resolution if evidence remains ambiguous. | T20, T21 |
| Parallel workers or retries exceed caps/duplicate outreach | Unique contact/sequence/job keys, atomic workspace quota reservations, leased jobs and guarded state transitions. No fresh send identity on ordinary retry. Unknown send consumes capacity until reconciled. | T06, T15, T20, T21 |
| Suppression erased by imports or identity churn | Durable workspace suppression registry checked at import, enrichment, enrollment and dispatch; merge aliases conservatively and retain suppression history. Ordinary deletes/reimports cannot silently resurrect eligibility. | T07, T18 |
| Stale approval or malicious recipient substitution | Immutable approval binds exact workspace, recipient/address, content and revision. Changes invalidate approval; dispatch verifies actor authority and current policy. No approval reuse across contacts or workspaces. | T14 |
| Development sends or uncontrolled automation | Environment hard block independent of UI/n8n; sending disabled by default; every message individually approved; workspace caps apply across campaigns; global stop precedes quotas and approval. | T01, T02, T15, T19 |
| Unauthorized price/contract action | AI drafts only using operator terms. Price changes, proposal send and contract acceptance require explicit human action; no AI-callable bypass. Preserve version and actor evidence. | T24, T25 |
| Stale inbound ingestion hides replies/stops | Durable ingestion/outbox monitoring and reconciliation; suspend dispatch if stop storage is unavailable or inbound freshness exceeds configured limit. Quarantine unmatchable replies until resolved. | T13, T22, T30 |
| Malicious HTML/content in UI and exhausted budgets | Render untrusted content safely; do not execute embedded markup/scripts. Bound source size, AI tokens, enrichment credits, retry count and job concurrency; cap failures in a visible review queue. | T10, T29, T31 |

## Recovery and residual risk
The operator can disable all workspace sends and revoke a compromised provider connection. Preserve audit evidence, queued job states, suppression and uncertain outcomes; restoring a backup or replaying events must not reactivate outreach. Test recovery with synthetic identities and fake providers, never live email.

No database transaction can atomically recall an external email. A reply may occur at the provider before its event is received locally, and a stop arriving after handoff may be too late for that message. Use timely ingestion, health gating and provider reconciliation to reduce this gap; show timing honestly instead of claiming exactly-once delivery or perfect stop timing.

Public research can be inaccurate and human reviewers can miss fabricated claims. Minimize collected data, show citations next to claims and avoid unsupported observations. Required provider signature, idempotency, retention and consent choices remain pending; a provider lacking reliable reply ingestion/reconciliation cannot pass the live-pilot gate.
